# Centennial Trucking: context for the CDL school site

Compiled Nov 12, 2026 from a read of centennialtrucking.com (8 pages) plus the discovery-call notes Jayce passed on. Facts below are what the pages said on Nov 12, 2026, not verified with the client.

## What the client asked for (discovery call, Nov 12, via Jayce)
- A NEW website for Centennial's truck-driving (CDL) school in Lindon, UT. Separate from centennialtrucking.com and not linked to it.
- Should feel as nice as centennialtrucking.com and use a similar red brand.
- Must NOT look AI-built ("when I see those kinds of things, it just looks stupid").
- Request-info form that emails Anna, who follows up leads by phone.
- Lead and traffic-source tracking (which ad / search / referral produced each lead).
- Optional pay-and-sign-up online. School is priced below market.
- SEO plus AI-search visibility (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity).
- Real photos: client provides; Jayce knows a photographer.
- Launching into trucking's slow season.
- Jayce promised a wireframe by about Nov 19.

### Tone (verbatim)
- "Built by People. Driven by Purpose." (About H1)
- "Reliable Heavy Civil & Infrastructure Hauling Solutions" (Home H1)
- "Every project runs on timing, and timing runs on trucking." (Services)
- "Driven by Reliability. Built on Trust." (Home)

Read: earnest, solid, plain-spoken, slightly corporate. The best line is the concrete one ("timing runs on trucking"); the weakest are the abstract slogan pairs.

### Brand
- Logo: black and silver wordmark with stylized truck outlines (per alt text).
- Red brand color: the client describes the brand as red. Exact hex values and fonts could NOT be read (the fetch returned page text, not CSS). Action: eyedrop the red and check fonts in the browser dev tools (a 2-minute job), or ask the client/RedRaven for the brand file.
- Imagery: appears to be real job-site and team photos (file names like "Excavator Transport on Lowboy Trailer", "SLC-airport-terminal"), plus simple line icons. One file is named for a general contractor (Sundt), so photo rights are worth confirming before reuse.

### Platform
- Built on Duda (inferred from the image CDN and meta tags, not confirmed), credited to the agency RedRaven AI in the footer.

### What's good (keep the spirit)
- Real trucks, real projects, real certifications: credibility comes from specifics, not adjectives.
- Clear CTA ("Get a Quote") and a contact form that asks qualifying questions.
- Complete titles, meta descriptions and canonical tags on every page.

### What's weak (don't copy)
- Abstract slogan headlines; unattributed testimonials; project list with no photos, dates or outcomes.
- Two "Get Estimate" buttons link to the homepage instead of the form.
- Same social share image on every page; one image alt text reads "Invisible Image".
- Careers page has no pay, requirements or path for someone without a CDL. That gap is exactly what the school fills: the school site can be the training pipeline the parent site never mentions.

## Open questions for the client (not answerable from the site)
- School's legal name and brand name (is it "Centennial ___ Driving School" or its own name?), and the Lindon street address.
- Is the school on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry (required since Feb 2022 for anyone delivering entry-level driver training)? This must be stated on the site.
- Programs offered (Class A, Class B, refresher, endorsements), hours/weeks, schedule (days/evenings/weekends), class size, price, what's included (DOT physical, permit, testing fees, truck for the skills test).
- Funding: WIOA / Utah DWS approved? Payment plans? Any hire-on or tuition reimbursement with Centennial Trucking itself?
- Job placement: does Centennial hire graduates? Pay ranges for local construction hauling vs OTR.
- Instructors: names, years driving, photos.
- Pass rates and graduate count, if they track them.
- Who receives leads besides Anna; what CRM, if any (Jayce: Velocity Forge uses GoHighLevel).
